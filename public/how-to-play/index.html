How to play

Sort Of, the hidden-stat ranking game

You get five things: drinks, planets, board-game squares, animals, books and more. They're ranked by a stat you can't see. Drag them into order from most (top) to least (bottom), then hit Lock it in.

Daily

One puzzle a day, the same for everyone. Instead of the stat, you get a short clue like “Sweet tooth.” Work out what the clue points to, then rank. You get one shot, and a new Daily arrives at midnight in your time zone. Missed one? Every finished Daily is in the archive with its answer.

Unlimited

  • Easy: you see the stat before you rank.
  • Normal: you get a clue. Stuck? Show a hint for 25 points and you'll see the unit it's measured in.

Play General for a mix of everything, or stick to one category: Food & Drink, Games, Sports, Animals & Body, Space, Around the US, Earth & Nature, Movies, TV & Books, Brands & Tech, Grab Bag, Timelines.

Scoring

  • 100 points if your #1 is right.
  • 75 points for each other item in exactly the right spot.
  • 25 points for each item that's just one spot off.
  • 400 is a perfect round.

Your squares show how close each guess was: green is exact, yellow is one spot off, orange is two, red is three or more.

Moving the cards

Drag a card up or down. You can also tap one card and then another to swap them, or use the arrow keys on a keyboard.

Tips

  • Lock in your #1 first. It's worth the most, and it's usually the easiest to spot.
  • Think about what the clue could mean before you rank. “Open wide” could be teeth, or mouths, or something else entirely.
  • Watch the units. A 12 oz can and a 20 oz bottle aren't a fair fight.

Where the numbers come from

Every stat is checked against a published source, like NASA, the U.S. Mint, governing-body rulebooks or official records, and the source is shown after each round. Numbers that can change over time, like championship counts or nutrition labels, show the date they were checked. Puzzles never put two numbers so close together that a small difference between sources could change the answer.
